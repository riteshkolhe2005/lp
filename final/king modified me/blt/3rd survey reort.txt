# SURVEY REPORT ON TYPES OF BLOCKCHAIN AND THEIR REAL-WORLD USE CASES

## Abstract

Blockchain is an emerging technology that provides a decentralized, transparent, and secure method of storing and sharing digital information. It maintains transaction records across multiple computers using cryptographic techniques, making unauthorized modification extremely difficult. Blockchain technology is widely used beyond cryptocurrencies in industries such as banking, healthcare, education, supply chain management, government, and real estate.

This survey report focuses on the four major types of blockchain: Public Blockchain, Private Blockchain, Consortium Blockchain, and Hybrid Blockchain. It examines their characteristics, advantages, limitations, and real-world applications. The report also compares different blockchain networks and discusses how blockchain technology improves security, transparency, efficiency, and trust in modern digital systems.

**Keywords:** Blockchain, Distributed Ledger Technology, Public Blockchain, Private Blockchain, Smart Contracts, Cryptocurrency, Decentralization.

## 1. Introduction

Blockchain is a distributed ledger technology that stores information in blocks connected together using cryptographic hashes. Each block contains transaction information, a timestamp, and the hash of the previous block.

Unlike traditional centralized databases, blockchain distributes transaction records across multiple nodes in a network. Transactions are validated through consensus mechanisms before being added to the blockchain.

Blockchain technology was initially introduced through Bitcoin in 2009. However, its applications have expanded into various industries, including financial services, healthcare, logistics, education, and digital identity management.

The main objective of blockchain is to provide a secure, transparent, and tamper-resistant system without requiring complete trust in a single central authority.

## 3. Types of Blockchain

Blockchain networks are mainly classified into four types based on accessibility, permissions, and network control.

### 3.1 Public Blockchain

A public blockchain is an open and decentralized network where anyone can participate in the network, view transactions, and validate transactions according to the network's rules.

**Characteristics:**
- Open to everyone.
- Highly decentralized.
- Transparent transaction records.
- No central authority controls the network.
- Uses consensus mechanisms such as Proof of Work and Proof of Stake.

**Examples:**
- Bitcoin
- Ethereum
- Litecoin

**Real-World Use Cases:**
1. Cryptocurrency transactions and digital payments.
2. Decentralized Finance (DeFi) applications.
3. NFT creation and digital ownership.
4. Decentralized applications (DApps).
5. Transparent fundraising through blockchain-based platforms.

### 3.2 Private Blockchain

A private blockchain is a permissioned network controlled by a single organization. Only authorized users can access, validate, and modify records according to predefined permissions.

**Characteristics:**
- Restricted access.
- Controlled by a single organization.
- Faster transaction processing.
- Better privacy and confidentiality.
- Suitable for enterprise applications.

**Examples:**
- Hyperledger Fabric
- Private Ethereum networks
- Enterprise blockchain implementations.

**Real-World Use Cases:**
1. Banking transaction management.
2. Internal company record management.
3. Secure employee information management.
4. Healthcare data management.
5. Enterprise supply chain tracking.

### 3.3 Consortium Blockchain

A consortium blockchain is a semi-decentralized network managed by multiple organizations instead of a single organization. Only selected participants can access and validate transactions.

It is particularly useful when multiple organizations need to share information securely.

**Characteristics:**
- Managed by multiple organizations.
- Restricted participation.
- Shared decision-making.
- Improved collaboration.
- Suitable for inter-organizational transactions.

**Examples:**
- Hyperledger Fabric consortium networks.
- R3 Corda networks.
- Multi-organization blockchain implementations.

**Real-World Use Cases:**
1. Interbank payment processing.
2. Supply chain management between manufacturers and suppliers.
3. International trade documentation.
4. Insurance claim verification.
5. Sharing research information between institutions.

### 3.4 Hybrid Blockchain

A hybrid blockchain combines the features of public and private blockchains. It allows organizations to maintain confidential information privately while making selected information publicly accessible.

**Characteristics:**
- Combination of public and private networks.
- Selective transparency.
- Controlled access to confidential information.
- Flexible data management.
- Suitable for applications requiring both privacy and transparency.

**Examples:**
- Dragonchain.
- XinFin (XDC Network).
- Custom enterprise hybrid blockchain implementations.

**Real-World Use Cases:**
1. Healthcare record management.
2. Real estate property registration.
3. Government record management.
4. Digital identity verification.
5. Secure business transactions.

## 4. Comparison of Different Types of Blockchain

| Features | Public | Private | Consortium | Hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Access | Open | Restricted | Restricted | Selective |
| Control | Decentralized | Single organization | Multiple organizations | Mixed |
| Transparency | High | Limited | Controlled | Selective |

| Speed | Generally lower | Generally high | Generally high | Depends on design |

## 5. Real-World Applications of Blockchain Technology

Blockchain is used in several industries to improve data security, transparency, traceability, and operational efficiency.

### 5.1 Banking and Finance

Blockchain enables secure digital transactions without depending entirely on traditional intermediaries.

**Applications:**
- International money transfers.
- Digital currencies.
- Smart contracts.
- Decentralized Finance (DeFi).
- Financial asset settlement.

**Example:** Bitcoin enables peer-to-peer digital currency transactions, while Ethereum supports decentralized financial applications.

### 5.2 Healthcare Industry

Blockchain helps maintain secure and verifiable healthcare records. It can support controlled sharing of patient information between hospitals and healthcare providers.

**Applications:**
- Electronic health record management.
- Medicine supply chain tracking.
- Medical data sharing.
- Clinical trial record management.
- Insurance claim processing.

**Example:** Blockchain-based systems can help pharmaceutical companies verify medicine authenticity and track products throughout the supply chain.

### 5.3 Supply Chain Management

Blockchain provides transparency and traceability throughout the product supply chain.

**Applications:**
- Product tracking.
- Verification of product origin.
- Prevention of counterfeit products.
- Inventory management.
- Monitoring transportation records.

**Example:** IBM Food Trust uses blockchain technology to improve food supply chain traceability and information sharing.

### 5.4 Education

Blockchain can provide secure and verifiable academic records.

**Applications:**
- Digital certificates.
- Online degree verification.
- Secure student records.
- Prevention of certificate forgery.
- Verification of academic qualifications.

**Example:** Universities can issue blockchain-based digital certificates that employers can independently verify.

### 5.5 Government and Public Services

Blockchain can improve transparency and accountability in government record management.

**Applications:**
- Digital identity management.
- Land ownership records.
- Public document verification.
- Government data management.
- Transparent public transactions.

**Example:** Blockchain-based land registration systems can provide tamper-resistant ownership records and simplify property verification.

### 5.6 Real Estate

Blockchain can simplify property transactions by maintaining transparent ownership and transaction records.

**Applications:**
- Property ownership verification.
- Digital property records.
- Smart contract-based agreements.
- Fractional property ownership.
- Secure property transactions.

**Example:** Tokenization allows property ownership to be represented digitally and potentially divided into smaller investment units, subject to applicable laws.

### 5.7 Internet of Things (IoT)

Blockchain can provide secure communication and transaction records between connected devices.

**Applications:**
- Secure device authentication.
- Smart home systems.
- Energy management.
- Device-to-device transactions.
- Industrial monitoring.

**Example:** Blockchain can help maintain trusted records of communication and transactions between IoT devices.

## 6. Advantages of Blockchain Technology

1. **Decentralization:** Reduces dependency on a single central authority.
2. **Transparency:** Provides a shared and verifiable transaction history.
3. **Security:** Uses cryptography and consensus mechanisms to protect records.
4. **Data Integrity:** Makes unauthorized changes to recorded information difficult.
5. **Traceability:** Helps track assets and transactions.
6. **Automation:** Smart contracts can automatically execute predefined conditions.
7. **Efficiency:** Can reduce intermediaries and manual verification processes.

## 7. Limitations and Challenges

1. **Scalability:** Some blockchain networks face limitations in transaction processing speed.
2. **Energy Consumption:** Certain consensus mechanisms, particularly Proof of Work, consume significant energy.
3. **Implementation Cost:** Developing and maintaining blockchain infrastructure can be expensive.
4. **Regulatory Issues:** Blockchain applications face different legal and regulatory requirements.
5. **Privacy Concerns:** Public blockchain transactions may expose sensitive information.
6. **Technical Complexity:** Blockchain implementation requires specialized technical knowledge.
7. **Irreversibility:** Incorrect transactions may be difficult or impossible to reverse.

## 8. Future Scope of Blockchain Technology

Blockchain technology has significant potential for future development. Its integration with emerging technologies can create more secure and efficient digital systems.

Some important future developments include:

- **Blockchain and Artificial Intelligence:** Secure sharing and verification of AI-related data.
- **Blockchain and IoT:** Improved security for connected devices.
- **Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDCs):** Development of digital forms of central bank money.
- **Digital Identity:** Secure and user-controlled identity verification.
- **Smart Contracts:** Increased automation of business processes.
- **Web 3.0:** Development of decentralized internet applications.
- **Tokenization:** Digital representation of real-world assets.

## 9. Conclusion

Blockchain technology is transforming the way digital information and transactions are managed. The four major types of blockchain, namely Public, Private, Consortium, and Hybrid, provide different levels of decentralization, privacy, accessibility, and control.

Public blockchains are suitable for open financial transactions and decentralized applications, while private blockchains are useful for organizations requiring controlled access and confidentiality. Consortium blockchains support collaboration between multiple organizations, whereas hybrid blockchains combine public transparency with private data management.

The survey indicates that blockchain has applications beyond cryptocurrency, particularly in banking, healthcare, supply chain management, education, government, and real estate. Despite challenges such as scalability, implementation costs, and regulatory concerns, blockchain has considerable potential to support secure, transparent, and efficient digital systems.
